Before and during the early part of my PhD I worked as a research assistant at the
[Ratio Institute](https://ratio.se), the [Research Institute of Industrial Economics (IFN)](https://www.ifn.se)
and the [Swedish Institute for Social Research (SOFI)](https://www.sofi.su.se).

Being the data-nerd that I am, I kept an archive of everything I contributed to.
Any work that thanked me for research assistance or comments is saved in a folder once it's out as a report, working paper, book or peer-reviewed article.
My archive holds roughly **50 projects** I worked on across the three institutes. The 20 below are the ones where I am explicitly thanked.
In the other works, I had a more minor part or it was a governmental report without any acknowledgements section.

Of the 20 works, 13 thank me for research assistance and 7 thank me for comments on the manuscript. Seventeen came out of my time at IFN, two from Ratio and one from SOFI.

The ten journal articles appeared in ten different outlets: the Journal of Finance, the Journal of
International Economics, the Journal of Industrial Economics, Energy Economics, Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice,
the Journal of Evolutionary Economics, the Journal of Institutional Economics,
the North American Journal of Economics and Finance, IZA World of Labor, and Ekonomisk Debatt.

<img src="/assets/img/research-ra-work/ra_works_by_year.svg" alt="Stacked bar chart of acknowledged works by year of publication, split into journal articles, books, working papers and reports" width="100%"/>

The years above are publication years, not the years I did the work.
The assistance took place between 2016 and 2021. The 2019 spike is the three
books plus two early IFN drafts. There's a long tail leading into 2025 and 2026 reflecting the journal review process.

<img src="/assets/img/research-ra-work/ra_works_by_field.svg" alt="Horizontal bar chart of acknowledged works by research field, led by entrepreneurship and energy economics" width="100%"/>

My favorite part about being an RA is the breadth of fields that I had the opportunity to assist in.
As an RA, I worked on Swedish register data, electricity market
institutions, patent data and much more.
The literature review on segregation was the starting point for my academic interest on the topic.

<img src="/assets/img/research-ra-work/ra_jel_footprint.svg" alt="Grid of the 18 top-level JEL categories, shaded by how many of the works declare codes in each; 14 of 18 are covered" width="100%"/>

One way to illustrate the breadths of topics covered is to illustrate the JEL codes in the published works. Nine of the twenty works declare JEL codes (the books and the Swedish-language pieces do not) and between them those nine touch
**14 of the 18 top-level JEL categories**. Only general economics, history of thought, macroeconomics
and economic systems are missing. Labor (J) and industrial organization (L) are the densest, which reflects the emphasis on applied microeconomics at the institutes where I was an RA.

## The researchers I worked for ##

In the projects that lead to a published work,
who were the researchers leading these projects?
Below is a co-authorship network for the published works that I was involved in. I've divided them up into three types of works, those where I'm (1) thanked for assistance, (2) thanked for comments, or (3) not thanked because my contribution was very minor or did not make it into the final product. 
This last category is not a rude omission of thanks, but if an RA just helped make a plot a bit nicer it does not require a full acknowledgement in the paper but rather a nice 'thank you' e-mail and a conversation around the coffee machine. 

The network below has one node per researcher, sized by how many of their projects I was involved in
and linked where two of them wrote together. Sometimes I know everyone of the co-authors, sometimes I know only one of them. I am not in this network at all, everyone here is here because I worked for them at some point.

## From working paper to journal ##

Several of the projects I assisted on were working papers when I left them.
Four of them have since appeared in peer-reviewed journals.
Below is an overview of the pipeline from working paper to publication.

| Working paper | Published as | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| IFN WP 1357 (2020) — *Economic Freedom and Antisemitism* | *Journal of Institutional Economics* (2021) | 1 year |
| Draft (2019) — *Taxation of Swedish Owner-Entrepreneurs, 1862 to 2018* | *Journal of Evolutionary Economics* (2023) | 4 years |
| IFN WP 1453 (2022) — *How International Experience Helps Shape Labor Market Outcomes* | *Journal of International Economics* (2026), retitled *From Local to Global* | 4 years |
| IFN WP 1201 (2018) — *What Is the Cost of Privatization for Workers?* | *Journal of Finance* (2025) | 7 years |

A median of four years from working paper to print, and seven for the one that landed in the *Journal
of Finance*. Six of the works below are still unpublished working papers.
